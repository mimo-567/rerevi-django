      How to mark a ‘d’ question in Eduqas GCSE Religious Studies – updated for 2025/26

Eduqas GCSE Religious Studies comprises two assessment objectives, each worth 50% of the
qualiﬁcation. Assessment Objective 2 is assessed solely through ‘d’ questions, which ask
candidates to analyse and evaluate a set statement. After each ‘d’ statement, there is the same
rubric for all ‘d’ questions except 2d on Route A Component 1 and 1d on Route B Component 1.
The rubric was updated in 2025/26 to make the language more accessible. This rubric is:

Discuss this statement.
Show that you have considered more than one point of view.
Refer to religious beliefs and teachings in your answer.
For 2d in Component 1 on Route A and 1d in Component 1 Route B, the rubric is as follows:

Discuss this statement.
Show that you have considered more than one point of view.
Refer to religious beliefs and teachings in your answer.
Refer to non-religious beliefs in your answer.
The rubric guides candidates towards what they should include in their responses, and the band
descriptors match accordingly (included below). When marking, it is essential to use these
descriptors to place responses into the correct band and then give a suitable mark.

                    Process for marking a ‘d’ question: a step-by-step guide

   1. Read the question and it’s rubric. As candidates should, look for key elements of the
      question that provide a more speciﬁc focus, such as ‘all’, ‘any’, ‘most’, ‘never’ etc. These
      give an indication of how the statement can be evaluated, e.g. rather than expecting a
      general evaluation of the pros and cons of abortion, if the statement was: ‘Abortion
      should only be allowed when the mother’s life is at risk.’, we would expect a focus on
      whether abortion is acceptable to save a mother’s life. Therefore, a general answer
      evaluating abortion is more likely to be placed in the lower bands whereas an answer
      focusing on whether abortion is acceptable to save the mother’s life, and whether this is
      the only acceptable reason or not, is more likely to be placed in higher bands.
   2. Read the indicative content included in the mark scheme. This is content that the
      Principal Examiner expected candidates to include in their answers. The guidance to
      credit any alternative answers is always included, as there may well be candidates who
      include excellent, accurate information that isn’t in the mark scheme, and they should
      not be penalised for doing so! In fact, it may well indicate an insightful response worthy
      of the higher bands (although this isn’t always the case so always refer to the band
      descriptors). Re-read this indicative content before each time before you begin to mark
      ‘d’ questions (which is also good advice for other types of questions).
   3. Re-read the band descriptors and have them out as you mark so that you can easily
      refer back to them.
   4. Read the answer carefully! Keep in your mind all of the things noted above: the speciﬁc
      focus of the question; indicative content; band descriptor content.
   5. Decide on a band. You may need to keep returning to look at the descriptors, especially
      for the ﬁrst few responses each time you mark a set of ‘d’ answers. Bands are ‘best ﬁt’.
6. Decide on a mark. Consider how far into the band the answer is: if it is close to the
    band above because it includes one or two points from the higher band then place it at
    the top of the band. Likewise, if it ﬁts that band but only just, place it at the bottom of the
    band.
7. On questions that require an additional mark for SPaG – mark SPaG after marking the
    answer. It is difficult to remember the content of an answer as well as the accuracy of
    SPaG and give both a fair and accurate mark at once. Mark the response itself ﬁrst,
    following the steps above, and then re-read it for SPaG.
8. Read the SPaG band descriptors (included below) and re-read the SPaG guidance
    booklet before each time you begin to mark a ‘d’ question that also needs to be marked
    for SPaG.
9. Highlight errors. Don’t highlight errors that could be a handwriting error rather than a
    SPaG error, such as a lower case ‘c’ for ‘Christians’. Keep in mind that it is a mark for
    SPaG, which means it is not just spelling that has to be considered.
10. Decide on a band using the guidance in the SPaG booklet.
11. Decide on a mark.
